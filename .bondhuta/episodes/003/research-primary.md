## Verified claims

- The update rule পর্ব ২ taught — `W_new = W + Error × X` — is a special case (η = 1, Error = d − y ∈ {−1,0,+1}) of the general perceptron-type learning rule `w ← w + η(d − y)x`. This general form is used consistently across modern ML teaching sources. — HIGH — form confirmed independently at multiple course/reference sources (Illinois CS440 lecture notes, GeeksforGeeks "What is the Perceptron Rule?", Sebastian Raschka's single-layer-neurons article) and matches scikit-learn's own implementation notes (below). No single canonical textbook page-quote was retrieved verbatim (see Could not establish), so treat the exact symbol choice (η vs α) as standard-practice, not a quoted textbook line.

- Historical origin: the algorithm traces to Frank Rosenblatt, described in Rosenblatt, F. (1958). "The Perceptron: A Probabilistic Model for Information Storage and Organization in the Brain." *Psychological Review*, 65(6), 386–408. DOI: 10.1037/h0042519. — HIGH — confirmed via PubMed (PMID 13602029) and MIT Press's Neurocomputing reprint listing. Do not use the word "perceptron" in-episode per brief; this citation is for internal consistency only.

- The first rigorous convergence proof for this class of algorithm is due to Albert B. J. Novikoff, not Rosenblatt himself: Novikoff, A. B. J. (1962). "On convergence proofs on perceptrons." *Proceedings of the Symposium on the Mathematical Theory of Automata*, Vol. 12, pp. 615–622, Polytechnic Institute of Brooklyn. — HIGH — corroborated by the Mohri & Rostamizadeh survey paper and by university course notes (Oregon State ML course, "Perceptron Mistake Bounds" arXiv:1305.0208). Note: a contemporaneous, more complicated proof is credited to H. D. Block (1962); Minsky & Papert's *Perceptrons* (1969) also attribute an earlier simple analysis to a 1961 Papert paper. Novikoff's is the version universally cited for the clean margin-based mistake bound.

- **Convergence theorem, exact conditions and guarantee** — HIGH — cross-verified from three independent readable sources: Mohri & Rostamizadeh, "Perceptron Mistake Bounds" (arXiv:1305.0208, also cs.nyu.edu/~mohri/pub/pmb.pdf); Oregon State University ML course notes ("Exploration 2.4: Perceptron Convergence Theorem and Proof"); and the search-aggregated statement of the Novikoff result. All three state the same theorem shape (see Exact statements below).

- The bound does **not** depend on the order in which the (finite, fixed) training examples are presented, nor on the total dataset size, nor on the input dimensionality — only on the geometric quantities R (radius) and γ/ρ/δ (margin), however they are named in a given source. — HIGH — stated explicitly in both the Mohri/Rostamizadeh-derived summary and the Oregon State notes ("Independent of dimensionality... Independent of example ordering (can iterate in any random sequence)"). IMPORTANT CAVEAT (own reasoning, not sourced beyond the theorem's own scope): "order-independent" here means the *worst-case bound on number of mistakes* holds no matter what order is used — it does NOT mean the trajectory of weights or the final converged W is identical across orders. The theorem is silent on which separator you land on; পর্ব ২'s own six epochs and one specific student order produced one particular converged W. This distinction must not be blurred in the episode.

- The standard proof of the theorem assumes **w₀ = 0 and η = 1** "for simplicity" — this is explicit in the Mohri & Rostamizadeh treatment as summarized by WebFetch extraction, and matches the Oregon State notes' stated assumption "initial weight 0". — HIGH for the zero/η=1 case specifically. The proof technique (norm lower-bound grows linearly in mistakes via margin, norm upper-bound grows as √mistakes via radius, contradiction forces a finite bound) is the same across all three sources.

- **Zero initialization + scaling of η**: with w₀ = 0, since every update is `w ← w + η·(d−y)x`, the entire weight trajectory after k updates is `w_k = η · Σ(updates with η=1)` — i.e. the whole sequence of weight vectors is a pure positive scalar multiple of the η=1 trajectory. Because the sign of `w·x` (and hence every prediction y, and hence which examples trigger an update, and in what order) is unchanged by multiplying w by a positive scalar, the entire sequence of correct/incorrect predictions is IDENTICAL for any η > 0 when w₀ = 0. Only the magnitude of the final weight vector scales with η. — HIGH, this is a direct algebraic consequence of the update rule's linearity and is not disputed by any source found; it is also implicit in why scikit-learn's `Perceptron()` fixes `eta0=1.0` by default (see next claim) and is stated in course-level treatments (e.g. the derivation `w(k) = w(0) + ηk·μ` reducing to `w(k)=ηk·μ` when w(0)=0, found in a perceptron theory course page, cse22-iiith.vlabs.ac.in). Confidence on this exact derivation as I state it: HIGH (it is straightforward, checkable linear algebra), though the specific secondary source quoting the `w(k)=w(0)+ηk·μ` form is course-notes tier (MEDIUM-tier source, used only as corroboration, not as the sole basis).

- scikit-learn's `Perceptron` estimator documents `eta0` as "Constant by which the updates are multiplied" with **default value 1**, and states in its own docs that `Perceptron()` is mathematically equivalent to `SGDClassifier(loss="perceptron", eta0=1, learning_rate="constant", penalty=None)`. — HIGH — directly fetched from scikit-learn 1.9 documentation, scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.linear_model.Perceptron.html. This is a level-1 (official docs) source and directly supports "η was silently 1 in পর্ব ২."

- **Nonzero initialization (পর্ব ২'s actual case, W₀ = [−3.0, 2.0, 1.0, 3.0])**: with w₀ ≠ 0, the trajectory is `w_k = w₀ + η · Σ(corrections)`. Because w₀ does NOT scale with η, the sign of `w_k · x` is in general no longer invariant to η — a larger η makes the accumulated corrections dominate w₀ faster; a smaller η lets w₀ (the "prior guess") continue to dominate predictions for longer. This means with nonzero w₀, η is not a cosmetic scale factor: it sets the relative weight of "what the teacher guessed at the start" versus "what the mistakes have argued for so far." — MEDIUM-HIGH: the algebraic decomposition `w_k = w₀ + η·Σcorrections` is exact and directly follows from the update rule (HIGH); the interpretive framing ("trading off prior guess vs. accumulated evidence") is my own reasoning from that algebra, not a quoted claim from any source, so treat the framing sentence itself as MEDIUM and the underlying algebra as HIGH.

- A general (non-zero-w₀) version of the mistake bound exists in the literature and is provable by the same two-sided-norm argument, but I could not retrieve an exact, citation-grade closed form (see Could not establish). What is HIGH confidence: the qualitative structure — the lower bound on progress is shifted by an initial-alignment term involving w₀ · v (v = the separating direction), and the upper bound on ‖w‖ growth is shifted by ‖w₀‖² — is described (at MEDIUM confidence, course/formalization-tier source) by a Coq-verified formalization of the theorem (Murphy, "Verified Perceptron Convergence Theorem," ACM CPP 2017/2018, dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3088525.3088673) which explicitly parameterizes the theorem over an arbitrary w₀. This is a peer-reviewed conference paper (ACM CPP), so it is a legitimate primary-tier source for "the theorem generalizes to nonzero w₀," but I did not extract the literal closed-form inequality with numeric certainty — do not put a specific formula from this in the episode without another verification pass.

- **η vs. gradient-descent learning rate — these are historically and mathematically DIFFERENT objects that later converged under one name.** The perceptron update rule (Rosenblatt 1958, formalized/analyzed by Novikoff 1962/Block 1962) predates and was NOT originally derived from gradient descent on a smooth loss — it is a direct, discrete "nudge toward correct" rule triggered only on a misclassification. Gradient descent's learning rate scales the negative gradient of a differentiable loss function with respect to weights. The perceptron's η scales a mistake-triggered correction term (d−y)x, and the "loss" for a 0/1-thresholded perceptron with the Error=(d−y) rule is not smooth/differentiable in the way gradient descent requires. Historically, the perceptron rule was later *shown* to be equivalent to running (sub)gradient descent on a particular non-differentiable "perceptron loss" — but that equivalence is itself later theory, not what Rosenblatt/Novikoff describe, and the episode brief explicitly forbids invoking gradient descent, derivatives, or slopes here (পর্ব ১০'s reveal). — HIGH confidence on "these are different historical objects with different original derivations"; the "later shown equivalent under one interpretation" fact is standard textbook material (widely stated, e.g. in ML course treatments of the "perceptron loss" as a special case of hinge loss with zero margin) but I did not pull a single primary citation for that specific equivalence in this pass — treat as MEDIUM and, per brief, do not use it in-episode anyway.

- **Interference is exact algebra, not folklore, and I verified it against পর্ব ২'s own printed numbers.** For the linear update `W ← W + η(d−y)x_B`, the score of any OTHER example A on the new weights changes by exactly `η(d_B − y_B)(x_B · x_A)` — the dot product of the two input vectors, scaled by B's error and η. — HIGH, direct algebra from the (already-verified) update rule; no external citation needed for the derivation itself, but I additionally checked it numerically against পর্ব ২'s printed Epoch-1 numbers (see Exact statements below) and it reproduces the Epoch-2 আবুল sum (0.31) printed on the page exactly.

- Condition for ZERO interference on A from B's update: either (a) `x_B · x_A = 0` (the two students' input vectors are orthogonal — in this dataset, with a bias input x₀=1 fixed at 1 for every student, this can never happen exactly, since the bias term alone contributes 1×1=1 to every dot product), or (b) B was already correctly classified, i.e. `d_B = y_B`, so no update fires at all (Error=0) — this is the case কাবুল and দাবুল hit repeatedly in পর্ব ২'s own log ("কোনো ভুল নেই → weight একটুও নড়লো না"). — HIGH, direct consequence of the rule; case (b) is literally printed in পর্ব ২'s existing text (lines ~661–676 of NeuralNetworkLearning.tsx, verified by direct file read).

- **"Catastrophic forgetting" / "catastrophic interference" is a named, established phenomenon in the cognitive-science and connectionist-network literature, first named by:** McCloskey, M., & Cohen, N. J. (1989). "Catastrophic interference in connectionist networks: The sequential learning problem." In G. H. Bower (Ed.), *The Psychology of Learning and Motivation*, Vol. 24, pp. 109–165. - **Is catastrophic forgetting the SAME thing as what happens in পর্ব ২'s single mistake-driven linear-threshold update, or a related-but-distinct phenomenon? — DISTINCT, and this distinction is important.** All three foundational catastrophic-forgetting papers (McCloskey & Cohen 1989, Ratcliff 1990, French 1999) study MULTI-LAYER, DISTRIBUTED connectionist networks trained with backpropagation on SEQUENTIALLY BLOCKED tasks (train fully on task A, then fully on task B, then test A) — where hidden-layer representations for different tasks overlap and interfere destructively, and where (unlike পর্ব ২'s single linear unit with a mistake bound) there is in general no convergence guarantee bounding how much is lost. পর্ব ২/৩'s setting is a single linear threshold unit, updated per-example (fully online, not blocked by task), where Novikoff's theorem GUARANTEES the total number of corrective mistakes is finite and bounded (if the data is linearly separable) — i.e., provable convergence to a fixed point exists for this simple system, which is precisely what is NOT true in general for the deep networks that exhibit "catastrophic" forgetting. — HIGH confidence on the distinction (architectural difference: single linear unit with online per-example updates vs. multi-layer distributed nets with blocked sequential task training) being real and citable from the papers' own scope statements; this is exactly the nuance the brief demands ("নড়ে, কিন্তু মোছে না" is honestly sayable for পর্ব ২/৩'s system in a way it is NOT for a deep net trained task-by-task).

- **Input scale and update magnitude.** Since the update is `Δw = η(d−y)x`, the size of the weight change is directly proportional to the magnitude (norm/components) of x. Scaling every input up or down by a constant factor scales the size of every mistake-triggered update by that same factor. — HIGH, direct algebra from the already-established rule.

- **However, scaling inputs is NOT simply cosmetic for the convergence bound, and NOT uniform across পর্ব ২'s actual input vector**, because পর্ব ২'s input vector includes a fixed bias input x₀ = 1 that is NOT divided by 100 (verified by direct file read of NeuralNetworkLearning.tsx, lines 288 and around 605–620: the vector is `[x₀=1(bias), x₁, x₂, x₃]` where x₁,x₂,x₃ are the ÷100-scaled percentages, but x₀ stays fixed at 1 regardless of scaling). This means uniformly rescaling only the feature inputs (not the bias) changes the RELATIVE size of the bias term's contribution to the dot product/update versus the feature terms' contributions — it is not a pure "everything scales together" operation. — HIGH (direct file verification) for the fact about the code; the consequence for R (the max-norm radius in Novikoff's bound) is: R = max‖x‖ is affected by whichever component (bias vs. features) currently dominates the vector norm, so ÷100-ing the features shrinks R (and shrinks each individual update step) but does not shrink the fixed bias component — this is a straightforward but real (and citable-only-to-the-rule-itself, HIGH) consequence.

## Exact statements and formulas

**The rule পর্ব ২ actually ran (η silently = 1):**
`W_new = W + Error × X`, Error = d − y ∈ {−1, 0, +1}
General form with η made explicit: `W ← W + η · (d − y) · X`, η = 1 in পর্ব ২.

**Novikoff's theorem (1962), standard modern statement** (cross-verified, Mohri & Rostamizadeh arXiv:1305.0208; Oregon State course notes):
Given a finite sequence of points x₁,...,x_T ∈ ℝⁿ with ‖x_t‖ ≤ R for all t, and assuming there exists a unit vector v (‖v‖=1) and margin γ > 0 such that y_t(v·x_t) ≥ γ for all t (i.e., the data is linearly separable by v with margin at least γ), then the perceptron algorithm, started at w₀ = 0 with η = 1, makes at most
**M ≤ R² / γ²**
mistakes (weight updates) in total, regardless of the order in which the T points are presented, and regardless of how many times each point recurs. Proof sketch (both sources agree): a lower bound ‖w_i‖ ≥ iγ grows linearly in the number of mistakes i (via margin), an upper bound ‖w_i‖² ≤ iR² grows only as √i (via radius), and these are incompatible past i = R²/γ², forcing termination.

**Verified numeric check of the interference formula against পর্ব ২'s own printed data** (own derivation, checked against file at src/pages/neural-network/NeuralNetworkLearning.tsx lines 610–664, 702):
- x_আবুল = [1, 0.60, 0.95, 0.20], x_বাবুল = [1, 0.30, 0.40, 0.90]
- After আবুলের own update, hypothetical আবুল-score with the just-updated weight = −0.25 + ‖x_আবুল‖² = −0.25 + (1²+0.60²+0.95²+0.20²) = −0.25 + 2.3025 = 2.0525
- বাবুলের update (Error = −1) changes W by −x_বাবুল. Its effect on আবুলের score (by the formula η(d_B−y_B)(x_B·x_A), here η=1, d_B−y_B=−1) is: −(x_বাবুল · x_আবুল) = −(1×1 + 0.30×0.60 + 0.40×0.95 + 0.90×0.20) = −(1 + 0.18 + 0.38 + 0.18) = −1.74
- 2.0525 − 1.74 = 0.3125, which rounds to **0.31** — exactly the আবুল sum printed at the start of Epoch 2 in পর্ব ২ ("আবুল : sum = 0.31"). This is an exact, checkable confirmation that the interference number for this dataset is −1.74 (a real, calculable "how much বাবুলের update moved আবুলের score by").

## Traps for the writer

- **Do not say "η doesn't matter" as a blanket statement.** It is only true (predictions/mistake-sequence invariant to η) when the initial weight vector is exactly zero. পর্ব ২ starts at W₀=[−3.0, 2.0, 1.0, 3.0], which is NOT zero — so in পর্ব ২'s actual run, η *would* have mattered had it been anything other than 1. Saying η is "just cosmetic" here would be false for this specific dataset/run.

- **Do not claim the mistake-bound's order-independence means "the final weights would be the same no matter what order we fed আবুল/বাবুল/কাবুল/দাবুল in."** The theorem bounds the worst-case *number* of mistakes across any order — it says nothing about the trajectory or the specific converged W being identical across orders. পর্ব ২'s converged W = [−3.0, 3.40, 3.10, 0.70] is a product of the specific order chosen; a different order is not guaranteed to land on the same numbers (though it is guaranteed to eventually stop making mistakes, if the data is linearly separable).

- **Do not conflate "catastrophic forgetting/interference" (McCloskey & Cohen 1989; Ratcliff 1990; French 1999) one-for-one with what happens to আবুলের score when বাবুলের update runs.** The named phenomenon is specifically documented in multi-layer, distributed, backprop-trained networks under blocked sequential task training, where there is generally no convergence guarantee. পর্ব ২/৩'s system is a single linear threshold unit with a mistake bound that provably terminates (given separability) — a materially different, better-behaved situation. Naming the grown-up phenomenon in one line (as the brief allows) is fine; implying it's literally the same math is a trap.

- **Do not claim ÷100 is "just" cosmetic scaling that affects nothing but arithmetic convenience.** It genuinely shrinks the magnitude of every update (Δw = η·Error·x, and x's feature components are 100× smaller after ÷100) and shrinks R in the mistake bound. But be careful not to overclaim uniform scale-invariance of the whole system either — the bias input x₀=1 is NOT divided by 100 in পর্ব ২'s actual vector, so the rescaling is partial, not global, and the relative weight of the bias term versus the feature terms changes as a result. A tutorial-style shortcut that says "scaling everything by the same factor changes nothing but units" would be wrong for this exact dataset because the bias component isn't part of "everything."

- **Do not derive or assert a numeric mistake-bound value (R²/γ² for this exact dataset) in the episode unless it is independently recomputed and checked** — I did not compute R and γ for the আবুল/বাবুল/কাবুল/দাবুল dataset in this pass, and the brief explicitly forbids introducing linear separability/margin language this episode anyway (পর্ব ৫ owns it). The Novikoff material here is for the writer's own confidence that "নড়ে না মোছে না" is honest, not for printing a mistake-count number.

- **Do not say the perceptron-type update IS gradient descent, or explain η through slopes/derivatives.** Per brief and per my research, the historical derivation is different; any equivalence to a loss-gradient view is later theory and is পর্ব ১০'s material.
